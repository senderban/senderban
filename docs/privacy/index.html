Privacy

Privacy policy

Senderban runs on your device. We never receive your mail data.

Effective 1 November 2026. Senderban is an early-release, open-source Windows app (senderban.com). This policy describes the code in the public repository, so you can check every statement against it.

No Senderban server

This version of Senderban has no server and no account with us. The app talks directly from your device to your mail provider (for Gmail, to Google; for Outlook and Microsoft 365, to Microsoft; for MXroute's blacklist, to the MXroute API). We do not receive, collect, store or see any of the data described below, and there is no analytics, telemetry, advertising or tracking in the app.

What the app handles, and where it stays

  • Mail account sign-in. For a mailbox reached over IMAP, the server address, your login and your password. The password is stored encrypted with the operating system's credential protection (Windows DPAPI, through Electron's safeStorage), on your device only.
  • Find my provider. When you press it, the app asks the DNS server your system uses about your address's mail domain, in ordinary unencrypted DNS, and may connect to that domain's mail server to ask what it is. The domain leaves your device; the rest of the address and your password do not. It is not stored or logged.
  • Sender and subject of new mail. Only the From address and the Subject of a newly arrived message, read on your device to show the notification, plus the server's X-Spam-Score header on IMAP accounts. An IMAP server's message envelope also carries the recipients; Senderban does not use or keep them. Senderban never reads or downloads message bodies or attachments.
  • MXroute API key (optional). Encrypted the same way, on your device only, and sent only to the MXroute API.
  • Your block list. The senders you chose to block, in a local database on your device, and written as rules to every mail account you added: a block applies to all of them. An Other (IMAP) mailbox gets no rule: the app itself moves that sender's mail out of its inbox while it runs, including mail of the last 30 days when you add the block. To do that it reads the sender of older messages in that inbox, on your device, and keeps nothing of them.
  • A diagnostic log. A local file on your device with events, states, error codes and the domain of a sender (not the full address). It does not contain passwords, tokens or subject lines. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you attach the file to a bug report yourself.

Google user data (Gmail)

You sign in to Gmail with your Google account, on Google's page in your browser. Senderban never sees your password, and the tokens Google issues stay on your device. The OAuth client that comes with the app identifies the app to Google. It is inside the installer, so it is no secret and it is the one part not in the repository; on its own it reads no mail, because Google asks for your consent first. As the owner of that Google Cloud project, we can see the totals Google shows there, such as the number of users and API traffic. Senderban asks for exactly two permissions:

  • View your email message metadata (gmail.metadata). To learn that new mail arrived and to show its sender and subject in the notification. This permission does not give access to message bodies.
  • Manage your basic mail settings (gmail.settings.basic). To create and delete the mail filters that block a sender you chose (future mail from that sender skips your inbox and goes to Trash, which you can undo).

What is stored on your device: your Gmail address and a refresh token, the token encrypted as described above. If you chose to use your own Google Cloud project, also that project's OAuth client ID and secret, the secret encrypted the same way. Access tokens are kept in memory only. Google user data is used only to provide these user-facing features and is not used for anything else.

Senderban's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Google user data is never sent to us or to any third party, with one exception that you trigger yourself: when you block a sender from a Gmail notification, that sender's address is also written as a rule to the other mail accounts you added to Senderban, because a block applies to all your accounts.
  • It is not used for advertising, and it is not sold.
  • It is not used to train or improve any machine-learning or AI model, and no AI service is used.
  • No person reads it: we never receive it.

Microsoft user data (Outlook and Microsoft 365)

Senderban signs in through Microsoft's own page and never sees your password. It asks for three permissions, and for a refresh token so that it stays signed in:

  • MailboxSettings.ReadWrite. To create, change and delete the Inbox rules named Senderban. It does not change any other rule.
  • Mail.ReadBasic. To find your Junk folder, and to read the sender, subject and time of new mail in your Inbox so the notification can offer to block the sender. They are read on your device and shown there; they are not stored and go nowhere else. This permission does not reach the body of a mail or its attachments.
  • User.Read. To learn your mail address.

What is stored on your device: your address, the refresh token, encrypted by Windows for your user, and Microsoft's marker for how far your Inbox was read. Access tokens are kept in memory only. The addresses and domains you block are written to Microsoft as rules, on your mailbox. Removing the account deletes the rules it can reach and the token, and tells you about any rule it could not delete or that you changed in Outlook; Microsoft keeps listing the access until you remove it at account.live.com/consent/Manage (personal account) or myapps.microsoft.com (work or school account).

Keeping and deleting your data

  • What is stored on your device is in Senderban's folder (under %APPDATA%, in a folder named Senderban). Deleting that folder removes it from your device.
  • Block rules live on your mail servers, not in that folder: unblock a sender in Senderban to remove them, before you remove the MXroute key if you used Block at server. Rules left behind stay until you delete them at your provider.
  • Removing a Gmail account in Senderban revokes Senderban's access at Google and deletes the stored token. You can also remove the access yourself at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Gmail filters that Senderban created stay in your Gmail until they are removed. Senderban is designed to remove its own filters when you remove the account; you can also delete them in Gmail settings, under Filters and Blocked Addresses.
