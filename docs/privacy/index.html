Privacy policy

Senderban runs on your device. Your mail data does not leave it.

Effective 26 October 2026. Senderban is a pre-alpha, open-source Windows app (senderban.com). This policy describes the code in the public repository, so you can check every statement against it.

No Senderban server

This version of Senderban has no server and no account with us. The app talks directly from your device to your mail provider (for Gmail, to Google). We do not receive, collect, store or see any of the data described below, and there is no analytics, telemetry, advertising or tracking in the app.

What the app handles, and where it stays

  • Mail account sign-in. For a mailbox reached over IMAP, the server address, your login and your password. The password is stored encrypted with the operating system's credential protection (Windows DPAPI, through Electron's safeStorage), on your device only.
  • Sender and subject of new mail. Only the From address and the Subject of a newly arrived message, read on your device to show the notification. Senderban never reads or downloads message bodies or attachments.
  • Your block list. The senders you chose to block, in a local database on your device.
  • A diagnostic log. A local file on your device with events, states, error codes and the domain of a sender (not the full address). It does not contain passwords, tokens or subject lines. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you attach the file to a bug report yourself.

Google user data (Gmail)

Gmail support (in development) uses your own Google Cloud project: you create an OAuth client there and enter its client ID and secret in Senderban. Senderban never sees any credentials but yours, and no one else holds access to your mail. It asks for exactly two permissions:

  • View your email message metadata (gmail.metadata). To learn that new mail arrived and to show its sender and subject in the notification. This permission does not give access to message bodies.
  • Manage your basic mail settings (gmail.settings.basic). To create and delete the mail filters that block a sender you chose (future mail from that sender skips your inbox and goes to Trash, which you can undo).

What is stored on your device: your Gmail address, the OAuth client ID and secret you entered, and a refresh token, the token and the secret both encrypted as described above. Access tokens are kept in memory only. Google user data is used only to provide these user-facing features and is not used for anything else.

Senderban's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Google user data is not transferred to anyone, because it never leaves your device.
  • It is not used for advertising, and it is not sold.
  • It is not used to train or improve any machine-learning or AI model, and no AI service is used.
  • No person reads it: we never receive it.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Everything above is stored in Senderban's folder on your device (under %APPDATA%, in a folder named Senderban). Deleting that folder removes it all.
  • Removing a Gmail account in Senderban revokes Senderban's access at Google and deletes the stored token. You can also remove the access yourself at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Gmail filters that Senderban created stay in your Gmail until they are removed. Senderban is designed to remove its own filters when you remove the account; you can also delete them in Gmail settings, under Filters and Blocked Addresses.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new text is published here and every change is visible in the public history. Questions: GitHub Issues. Security reports: use private vulnerability reporting on the repository, as described in its security policy.
