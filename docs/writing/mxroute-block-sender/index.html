How do I block a sender on MXroute, and what does each way cover?

To block a sender on MXroute, add the address, or *@example.com for a whole domain, to the blacklist in the MXroute control panel. MXroute says that list covers every domain on your account and is not a guaranteed refusal. The other way is a Sieve rule: it acts on one mailbox and files the mail into Junk or Trash.

Key facts

  • MXroute's blacklist takes a full address or *@example.com. Its documentation, read on 2 November 2026, says the sender lists "apply across the hosting account's domains".
  • On one MXroute mailbox on 25 October 2026, mail from a blacklisted address was refused with 550 High scoring spam message has been dropped, and no copy was kept. MXroute says a blacklist entry "is not a guaranteed SMTP-level rejection".
  • A Sieve rule acts on one mailbox, at delivery. Senderban's rule for the domain brand.example, run on Dovecot 2.4.5 on 2 November 2026, filed 4 of 4 senders at the domain or under it into Junk and left 4 of 4 look-alike senders in the inbox.
  • On a real MXroute mailbox, Senderban's rule was written 1.9 s after the click (25 October 2026), and the blocked sender's next mail was filed away (30 October 2026).
  • Senderban never puts a whole domain on the MXroute blacklist. Refusal at the server is for one address at a time, and it is off until you switch it on.

Where can I block a sender on MXroute?

In two places, and they cover different things. The blacklist sits in MXroute's spam check, before a mail is delivered, and it is one list for the whole hosting account. A Sieve rule sits in one mailbox and runs as each mail is delivered there. MXroute's documentation (read 2 November 2026) has a page for the blacklist and none for Sieve. Senderban reaches an MXroute mailbox's Sieve scripts over ManageSieve, the standard protocol for them, and read its own rule back from a real MXroute mailbox on 25 October 2026 (ADR 0003).

Question Blacklist Sieve rule
Where you set it MXroute control panel, or the MXroute API A Sieve script on the mailbox, written through ManageSieve
What it covers Every domain on the hosting account, by MXroute's documentation One mailbox
What happens to the mail An input to spam scoring. Refused with a 550 reply in our one test; MXroute does not promise that Filed into the folder the rule names, such as Junk or Trash
A whole domain *@example.com address :domain :is "from" "example.com"

How do I block a sender with the MXroute blacklist?

These are the steps on MXroute's Spam Filters and Sender Lists page, read 2 November 2026. Put your sender where this says example.com.

  1. Open the Control Panel: Management, Subscriptions, then open the control panel for the service.
  2. Go to Spam Filters, then Manage Lists under Whitelist & Blacklist.
  3. Under Add New Entry, type the sender in Email Address or Pattern: a full address such as news@example.com, or *@example.com for every sender at that domain.
  4. In Add to, choose Blacklist (Blocked).
  5. Click Add Entry, and check that the entry is in the blacklist.
*@example.com

Keep the domain after the @ whole: the same page says SpamAssassin "does not properly process entries with * after @", and it asks you to keep broad patterns and lists small. To undo an entry: Spam Filters, Manage Lists, the entry's Delete icon, then Delete in Confirm Deletion.

With an API key the same list is POST /domains/{domain}/spam/blacklist with a body of {"entry": "..."}, at most 254 characters, in MXroute's API description (read 2 November 2026). Writes are limited to 60 requests a minute.

What does the MXroute blacklist cover, and does it refuse the mail?

It covers the whole hosting account, and it may or may not refuse the mail. Both of MXroute's own documents say the list is shared by every domain on the account, even though the API names one domain in its path. The list feeds the MXroute spam filter, and MXroute does not tie an entry to one outcome.

Question Answer Source
Which mailboxes does an entry cover? "These lists apply across the hosting account's domains." MXroute panel docs, read 2 Nov 2026
Is that also true through the API? "Sender lists are shared across an account's domains." MXroute API description, read 2 Nov 2026
Is the mail refused? "A blacklist entry is not a guaranteed SMTP-level rejection." The lists are "sender-list inputs to spam processing". MXroute panel docs
What decides it? The High Score Threshold, a whole number from 1 to 50; MXroute recommends 15. The API calls it the "score threshold for auto-deletion". MXroute panel docs and API description
What did we see? 550 High scoring spam message has been dropped, no copy kept, and Unblock took the entry off again One MXroute mailbox, 25 Oct 2026 (ADR 0008)

One consequence matters more than the rest. A From address can be forged, and the list is shared. An entry made because of one forged mail refuses the real sender's mail for every domain and mailbox on the account, and a refused mail leaves no copy to find. That is why Senderban never offers refusal for your own addresses or for an address on the mail domain of any account it knows (ADR 0008, item 3).

How does a Sieve filter block a sender on one mailbox?

A Sieve rule is a short script on the mail server that runs on each mail as it is delivered to that mailbox. The Sieve standard, RFC 5228, describes "a language for filtering email messages at time of final delivery". So the rule moves the next mail from a sender, not the mail already in your inbox, and it works with every mail app and on your phone, because the server runs it.

This is the rule Senderban wrote for a test mailbox on 2 November 2026, for one address and one whole domain, read back from the server as it was stored: 

require ["fileinto", "mailbox", "envelope"];
# senderban-managed v1
if anyof (
  address :is "from" [
    "smith@example.com"
  ],
  address :domain :is "from" [
    "brand.example"
  ],
  address :domain :matches "from" [
    "*.brand.example"
  ],
  envelope :is "from" [
    "smith@example.com"
  ],
  envelope :domain :is "from" [
    "brand.example"
  ],
  envelope :domain :matches "from" [
    "*.brand.example"
  ]
) {
  fileinto :create "Junk";
  stop;
}
  • address ... "from" reads the From header. envelope ... "from" reads the sender the sending server gave, which can differ from the header. Each key is tested against both.
  • :domain :is "brand.example" compares the whole domain part of the address, not a piece of it. :domain :matches "*.brand.example" adds the subdomains. Without a comparator, Sieve compares without regard to upper and lower case (RFC 5228, section 2.7.3).
  • fileinto :create "Junk" files the mail into Junk and makes the folder if it is missing; stop ends the script for that mail.

On MXroute, look for that mail in Junk. MXroute's default folders page (read 2 November 2026) lists Junk as not subscribed by default, and says a mail app that shows only subscribed folders may need you to subscribe to Junk to see it.

Does a Sieve domain block catch look-alike domains?

Not in our test. The rule above filed every sender at brand.example or under it into Junk, and left a longer domain, a domain that only starts the same way and a display name holding the domain in the inbox. That is the difference from Gmail, where the same kind of block also caught brand.example.test (our Gmail measurement).

Twelve mails, twelve results as meant: 5 into Junk, 7 left in the inbox. The fourth row is the envelope test at work: the header looked harmless, and the sending server's envelope sender was under the blocked domain.

What went wrong in our own Sieve rules?

Two drafts of Senderban's spam-score rule failed on a real Sieve server, Pigeonhole 2.4.5, on 31 October 2026 (ADR 0017). They apply to any rule you write by hand.

What we wrote What the server did What we write now
? for one character before a * 1? * matched 1.6 (+), and ?.6 * matched 4.9 (++++): the ? did not hold one character Digits, then at most one * at the end
:value "ge" with the i;ascii-numeric comparator A value with no leading digit counts as positive infinity (RFC 4790, 9.1.1), so a clean mail's -1.2 was over every limit No numeric comparator; a list of patterns
Two header tests, the second to rule out the first's mistake Each test can be met by a different line of the same header: -1.2 beside 0.0 moved clean mail One test on one list, so one line has to hold all of it

The third trap applies to any rule that tests the same header twice: a header that appears on two lines can meet each test with a different line. A Sieve rule is not finished until a real server has delivered a mail through it.

What does Senderban do on MXroute, and what does it not do?

Senderban is a free, open-source Windows app. On an MXroute mailbox it shows a notification for new mail with three choices: Move to Junk (the default), Move to Trash and, if you switched it on, Block at server. Junk and Trash write the Sieve rule shown above, for the sender or the whole domain, and read it back. Block at server puts one address on the MXroute blacklist and keeps a Junk rule as the backup for a mail that still arrives.

Choice Writes Whole domain Copy of the mail
Move to Junk Sieve rule on the mailbox Yes In Junk
Move to Trash Sieve rule on the mailbox Yes In Trash
Block at server Blacklist entry, plus a Junk rule No, one address None if the server refuses it

Measured on a real MXroute mailbox: the rule was written 1.9 s after the click on 25 October 2026, and read back from the server (ADR 0003), and on 30 October 2026 the whole loop ran: new mail, notification, block, the next mail filed away, undo (README).

What it does not do on MXroute, as the README and ADRs state for v0.2.0:

  • No whole domain on the blacklist. Block at server takes single addresses only, and Senderban never writes or deletes a blacklist entry that holds a * or starts with @. A domain is blocked by the Sieve rule.
  • No refusal by default. Block at server needs an MXroute API key and a switch on each mail domain, and it is never offered for your own addresses or mail domains.
  • No change to your spam settings. Senderban's MXroute client calls the domain list and the blacklist, nothing else; your High Score Threshold stays as it is.
  • Your own blacklist entries are not safe from Unblock. The blacklist has no tag, so Unblock removes an address there even if you had listed it yourself.
  • Removing a mailbox leaves the blacklist. Senders Senderban put there stay until you delete them in the MXroute panel.
  • Not on mail already in the inbox. The rule acts at delivery.

What was not measured?

  • A whole-domain Sieve rule on MXroute itself. The look-alike results come from Dovecot 2.4.5; on MXroute a single-address rule was measured.
  • A *@example.com entry on the MXroute blacklist, and whether an entry made under one domain refuses mail to a second domain on the account. MXroute's documentation says it does; we tested one domain.
  • Whether a blacklisted sender is refused at every High Score Threshold. The 550 came from one mailbox with the threshold it had.
  • On MXroute: adding Senderban's rule to a Sieve script you already run, and a match on the envelope sender alone (the test mail had the same From and envelope address).
  • Forged mail from a domain on your own MXroute account, and whether MXroute's checks stop it.
  • Whether MXroute changes any of this later: the dates above are the dates it held.
