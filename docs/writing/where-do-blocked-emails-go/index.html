Where do blocked emails go, and does the sender know?

Blocked emails usually still arrive: Gmail files them in Spam, Outlook in the Junk Email folder, and Apple Mail on a Mac leaves them in the Inbox or moves them to the Trash, as you choose. Whether the sender is told is not documented: none of the Google, Microsoft or Apple help pages we read says so either way. The one reply we saw go back was from a refusal at the mail server: a 550 error, on MXroute.

Key facts

  • Gmail's Block sends a sender's future mail to Spam; Outlook's moves it to the Junk Email folder (help pages, read 11 November 2026).
  • New Outlook and Outlook on the web remove mail from Junk after 14 days; the classic Outlook steps give no period. iCloud deletes mail in Junk after 30 days. The Google pages read do not say how long Gmail keeps Spam.
  • Whether a blocked sender is told is not documented on any of the 10 Google, Microsoft and Apple pages read on 11 November 2026.
  • On one MXroute mailbox on 25 October 2026, a block at the server refused the mail: the sending side got 550 High scoring spam message has been dropped, and no copy was kept.
  • Senderban runs on Windows today. The providers' own blocks on this page need no extra app.

Where do blocked emails go?

Into a folder in your own mailbox, on the pages read. The block decides where the mail lands; it does not stop the sender from sending it. One Microsoft page says otherwise for Outlook.com (below). This is what each provider's help page says about its own block, read on 11 November 2026. None of these blocks was run for this post.

Where you block Where the mail goes Source
Gmail, Block "⁠{sender}⁠" Spam Google, Block an email address in Gmail
Outlook, Blocked senders Junk Email folder Microsoft, Block or unblock senders in Outlook
Mail on iPhone or iPad, Block this Contact The trash folder Apple, Block phone numbers, contacts, and emails
Mail on Mac, Blocked settings The Inbox, marked as blocked, or the Trash: your choice Apple, Change Blocked settings in Mail on Mac

The Mac setting is under Mail, Settings, Junk Mail, Blocked. Apple's page gives two choices: "Mark as blocked mail, but leave it in my Inbox", which shows a banner on the message, and "Move it to the Trash", which "Automatically move[s] messages from blocked senders to the Trash mailbox". Apple's iPhone User Guide page on flagging or blocking emails says a block is "to prevent a particular sender from emailing you in the future", and does not say where the mail goes; the support article in the table does.

Do blocked emails go to spam?

In Gmail, yes: "After you block the sender, all future emails from them go to Spam". Google's page on spam in Gmail adds that "When you block a sender, even when you remove their emails from Spam, Gmail still automatically identifies their emails as spam". In Outlook they go to the Junk Email folder, Outlook's spam folder. Apple's iPhone article says blocked mail goes to the trash folder; Apple's Mac page lists the Trash or the Inbox as a choice, and does not say which is the default.

So can a blocked email still email you? On Outlook, Microsoft's page says so in so many words, of classic Outlook: "The blocked person can still send you mail, but if anything from his or her email address makes it to your email account, it's immediately moved to the Junk Email folder." The same page says the Junk Email Filter "doesn't stop junk email from being delivered". A second Microsoft page, Receiving email from blocked senders in Outlook.com, says instead that "Blocking someone stops their email from coming to your mailbox". The two pages do not agree, and we did not test Outlook's own block to see which holds.

If a blocked sender's mail is in your inbox rather than in one of these folders, that is a different problem, with four usual causes: Why are blocked emails still coming through?

If you block an email address, does the sender know?

Not documented. We read 10 help pages from Google, Microsoft and Apple on 11 November 2026, every help page linked in this post, and none of them says whether a person you block by email is told. This post does not claim either answer.

Provider Pages read Sender told about an email block?
Google, Gmail 3 Not documented
Microsoft, Outlook and Outlook.com 2 Not documented
Apple, Mail and iCloud 5 Not documented for email; Apple's page says the sender is not told, for calls and messages only

The Apple row needs care. Apple's page on blocking contacts, read 11 November 2026, says of a blocked phone number or contact: "the contact won't get a notification that the call or message was blocked". The sentence about email follows it: "When you block an email address from Mail, it goes to the trash folder." It says nothing about telling the sender, so the first sentence cannot be stretched to cover email.

What most of the pages read do say is that a filing block accepts the mail. Gmail files it in Spam, Outlook in Junk, Apple Mail in the Trash or the Inbox: in each case the mail reaches your mailbox. The exception is Microsoft's Outlook.com page, which says blocking "stops their email from coming to your mailbox"; we did not test which holds. A refusal at the server is different: the server turns the mail away and replies to the sending server. That is the one outcome on this page we measured.

How long is blocked mail kept?

That depends on the folder, and not every page says. These are the retention periods stated on the help pages, read on 11 November 2026.

Folder Kept for The page's words
New Outlook and Outlook on the web, Junk Email 14 days "automatically removed from the Junk Email folder after 14 days and can't be recovered after that"
iCloud, Junk 30 days "Email messages in the Junk folder automatically delete after 30 days"
Gmail, Trash 30 days "Up to 30 days after deletion: You can find the message in Trash" and "After 30 days: The message is permanently deleted"
Gmail, Spam Not stated The three Google pages read give no period for Spam

Sources: Outlook from Microsoft's blocking page, whose new Outlook and Outlook on the web sections state the 14 days and whose classic Outlook section gives no period; iCloud from Apple's Report and reduce spam in iCloud Mail and Manage junk mail in Mail on iCloud.com, which says Junk is "automatically deleted after 30 days"; Gmail's Trash from Google's page on deleting and recovering messages. If you want to check a blocked sender's mail, check it within the shortest of these.

Filed in a folder or refused: what is the difference?

A block files the mail or refuses it. Filing is what the provider buttons above do: the server accepts the mail and puts it in a folder, so you can still find it until that folder is emptied or the provider's retention above runs out. Refusing turns the mail away at the receiving server: in our test it answered the sending server with an error code and kept nothing.

We measured a refusal once. On 25 October 2026, on one MXroute mailbox, Senderban put a sender's address on MXroute's blacklist from its notification (then a choice called Block at server; in 0.3.0 it is the notification's Block button, shown once Block at server is switched on in Settings). Mail from that address was then refused with 550 High scoring spam message has been dropped, and the mailbox kept no copy. Unblock removed the entry again. That 550 reply goes to the sending server, the only signal in this post that leaves your mailbox. Whether the person who wrote the mail then saw a returned-mail notice was not observed.

Question Filed in a folder Refused at the server
Who does it Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail; Senderban by default Senderban's Block at server, MXroute only
Is a copy kept? Yes, until the folder is emptied or the provider's retention runs out No (measured 25 October 2026)
Reply to the sending server Not documented on the pages read 550 High scoring spam message has been dropped
Can you undo it for past mail? Yes, move the mail back before the folder's retention runs out No: refused mail is gone

The refusal is not guaranteed. It depends on the spam-score limit in the MXroute panel, so Senderban also keeps a Junk rule for a mail that still gets through. More on MXroute's two ways to block: How do I block a sender on MXroute, and what does each way cover?

Where does Senderban put a blocked sender's mail?

Senderban is a free, open-source Windows app. It shows a notification for new mail, and one click writes the block on the provider's server where the provider lets a program write a rule. Where it cannot, it moves the mail itself while it runs. It does not send mail, so it never writes to the blocked sender. Each choice below is from the README and ADR 0013, for v0.3.0.

Provider Where the mail goes Measured on a real account
Gmail / Workspace Trash: a Gmail filter cannot file mail into Spam Yes, 30 October 2026
Outlook / Hotmail / Microsoft 365 Junk or Deleted Items Personal account only, 1 November 2026: mail went to Deleted Items
MXroute Junk (default) or Trash; Block at server refuses one address Yes: filing 30 October 2026, refusal 25 October 2026
Other ManageSieve servers Junk (default) or Trash No: a local Dovecot server only
iCloud, Yahoo, AOL, GMX, Web.de Junk (default) or Trash, moved by the app while it runs iCloud only, 1 November 2026: 3 mails moved; the others on Dovecot only

What it does not do, as the README and ADRs state for v0.3.0:

  • No Spam on Gmail. A Gmail entry goes to Trash, which Gmail keeps for up to 30 days. Gmail's own Block, above, is the way to send a sender to Spam.
  • No refusal except on MXroute, for single addresses, off until you switch it on. Everywhere else a Senderban block files the mail.
  • No rule on iCloud, Yahoo, AOL, GMX or Web.de. With Senderban closed, a blocked sender's mail stays in the inbox.
  • No permanent delete by Senderban. Junk and Trash can be undone until the provider's own retention runs out (14 days for Junk in new Outlook and Outlook on the web, 30 days for iCloud Junk). Block at server is the one choice where Senderban itself leaves no copy, and you have to pick it.

If you use one provider, its own block above does the same filing without Senderban. What Senderban adds is the block from the notification, one list for every account you add, and the refusal on MXroute. See where it works.

What was not measured?

  • The providers' own blocks: Gmail's Block, Outlook's Blocked senders, Block this Contact on iPhone and the Mac's Blocked settings were not run. What they do here is what the pages say.
  • Whether a sender sees anything after a filing block. No sender's mailbox was watched.
  • Whether the person who sent a refused mail saw a returned-mail notice after the 550. That is up to the sending side, which was not observed.
  • How long Gmail keeps Spam, how long Outlook keeps Deleted Items, and how long iCloud keeps Trash: the pages read do not say.
  • Which of Microsoft's two Outlook.com statements holds: Junk, or not reaching the mailbox at all.
  • Other MXroute accounts, and the refusal at other spam-score limits. Each measurement here is one account on one day.
