Why blocking @paypal.co on Outlook would also block @paypal.com

Outlook keeps a domain block as a text match. A look-alike phisher's domain can be the first letters of the real sender's, so blocking the phisher would silence the real one. This was found in review before the Outlook lane shipped. Here is the mechanism, what Senderban refuses now, and what that costs.

A domain block on Outlook is text

Outlook.com and Microsoft 365 have no Sieve, and Microsoft Graph v1.0 has no call that writes the blocked-senders list. What stays is an Inbox rule, a messageRule (ADR 0013). One address has an exact condition, fromAddresses. A domain has none: of the 30 conditions in messageRulePredicates, the one that fits is senderContains, "the strings that appear in the from property of an incoming message". So Senderban writes a domain and its subdomains like this: 

"senderContains": ["@example.org", ".example.org"]

@example.org catches every address at that domain, and .example.org its subdomains. Both also catch any longer domain that starts with the same letters: @example.org.tr is somebody else, and its mail matches too. ADR 0013 lists that as a known limit, and the mail goes to Junk or Deleted Items, where it can be recovered.

The case that matters: blocking the phisher

On 1 November 2026 a red-team pass over the code, a review that plays the attacker, asked what happens when the person does the right thing. A phishing mail arrives from @paypal.co, a look-alike. The person blocks the whole domain from the notification. The rule says "@paypal.co", and every address at @paypal.com contains that text. The real sender's alerts now go to Junk, in the middle of an attack that imitates that sender.

The guards at the block list did not see it. They compare domains label by label, and the two names share no label boundary. The same holds for .ne before .net, .in before .info and .bi before .biz, and @amazon.co also matches @amazon.co.uk. The finding was rated medium and dormant: the build had no Microsoft client ID yet, so no Outlook lane could run. The note said to treat it as blocking before one was set.

What was measured, and what was not

  • Microsoft's reference defines senderContains as strings that appear in the sender, and lists no exact-domain condition.
  • On one personal Outlook.com mailbox on 1 November 2026, a domain block was written as senderContains: "@domain" (POST 201), and mail from another address of the blocked domain did not reach the Inbox (ADR 0013, M3).
  • Not reproduced on a mailbox: a look-alike rule catching the real domain's mail. It follows from the documented definition, and the fix stops Senderban from writing such a rule, so there was nothing left to try it with.

The decision: refuse it on Outlook only

The Outlook lane now refuses a domain right under .co, .ne, .in or .bi, the top-level domains that are the first letters of com, net, info and biz. That covers @paypal.co, @mail.paypal.co and such a suffix on its own. A name under a longer suffix is still written: @spam.co.in could only also reach @spam.co.info, where no real sender lives. The older guards stay: a domain whose text would reach the person's own mail, a listed mail provider, or the account alerts of Google, Microsoft or Apple is refused too.

A refused entry stays on the device list, which says Outlook: not applied, and the result notification says why. Sieve servers compare the whole domain (address :domain :is) and the IMAP cleaner compares labels, so those mailboxes still apply it. The 0.2.0 changelog tells people to block the address instead.

The tests hold ten look-alike pairs. Each one checks that the look-alike really is the start of the real name, that the guard refuses it, and that a fake Graph server ends with no rule. Three names under a longer suffix check the opposite: one rule each. Source: graph/adapter.ts, its tests.

What it costs

  • A spammer who really lives under .co cannot be blocked by domain on Outlook. Their address still can.
  • The guard is narrow on purpose. Every top-level domain that starts another would be 134 of the 248 country codes, by the count in the code against IANA's list of 1 November 2026, and refusing all of them would refuse most domain blocks. So .com still reaches .com.tr, and .ba still reaches .bank. The notification names that limit, and the mail stays in Junk or Deleted Items. This was accepted after the security review the same day.
  • Still open: whether senderContains also reads the display name is not documented. It is on the list to measure (ADR 0013, M3).

The general lesson: when a provider offers only a text match, the dangerous case is not the spammer you meant to block, it is the trusted name that starts with the same letters.
