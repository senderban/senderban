            How Block at server works

            New-mail notifications then offer “Block” for one sender: the server refuses their mail at the door, so the sender gets an error and the mail does not reach Junk or Trash. Junk and Trash stay available and never touch MXroute. MXroute says its blacklist applies across your hosting account's domains, so a refused address may be refused for the mailboxes of all those domains, including any where Block at server is off. An address already on your blacklist is treated as Senderban's own and taken off again when you unblock it. A whole domain is never refused at the server. Whether MXroute drops a sender also depends on the spam-score limit in your MXroute panel, so try one block and check. Takes effect when Senderban starts; switching it off takes back what Senderban added.

              Use my own Google project (advanced)

              Senderban signs in through your own Google Cloud project instead. In that project turn on the Gmail API, make an OAuth client of type Desktop app, and paste its ID and secret here. The secret is stored encrypted on this device.

              For Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live, MSN and Microsoft 365. Senderban opens Microsoft's sign-in page in your browser and never sees your password. It asks to change mailbox rules, to find your Junk folder, to read the sender and subject of new mail for the notification, and to read your address. It never reads the body of a mail. A work or school account may need your organization's approval first.
